GV Tools 0.65.0 — what changed from 0.50.0

Adapted 9 November 2026 from the reviewed 0.60.0 candidate. Every change, why it was made, and how the 0.65.0 results differ from 0.50.0.

Products change value. Parsivel concentrations, N(D), LWC, Dm, σm, Nw and moments change because 0.50.0 divided the counts by the wrong fall speed. Rain rate and Z change by about 9 % and 0.8 dB because 0.65.0 uses the OTT size classes (§2.2). Don't mix 0.50.0 and 0.65.0 Parsivel products in one analysis.

2. Scientific corrections

science2.1 Parsivel number concentration used the wrong fall speed

Where
src/gv_tools/_parsivel.py — get_integral_parameters (0.50.0) → integral_parameters (0.65.0)
0.50.0
denominator = 60 * A[:, None] * Theoretical_Vt[None, :]. Theoretical_Vt is the terminal speed at each diameter class, but it was broadcast along the velocity axis. A drop in cell (Di, Vj) was divided by Vt(Dj), the terminal speed of the diameter class that shares the velocity index. A 1 mm drop counted in the 3.0 m/s class was divided by 8.8 m/s, the speed of a 3.9 mm drop, so small drops were undercounted roughly threefold.
0.65.0
N(Di) = (1/ΔDi) Σj nij / (Ai Δt Vij). Vij is the measured class centre Vj by default (Tokay et al. 2014; Jaffrain & Berne 2011), or Vt(Di) with fall_speed="theoretical". Concentration, LWC, Z, moments, Dm, σm and Nw all follow from N(D). Rain rate is the volume flux through the beam, so it is unaffected (it was already correct).
Effect
Concentration and LWC rise, Dm and σm fall (§7). Each formula is checked against an independent loop implementation in tests/test_parsivel_decoder.py.

science2.2 Size classes: OTT manual values by default

0.50.0
parsivel_diameter_py.txt lists every diameter class centre and width multiplied by exactly 1.03 (0.064 instead of 0.062 mm … 25.235 instead of 24.5 mm). Between 0.85 and 3.8 m/s its Measured_Vt column also differs from the OTT velocity classes, by up to 9 %. Neither change is documented.
0.65.0
classes="ott" (default) uses the OTT Parsivel² class centres and widths bundled in diameter.dat/velocity.dat. classes="legacy" reproduces the 0.50.0 tables.
Effect
A 3 % size scaling raises R by 1.03³ ≈ 9.3 % and Z by 1.03⁶ ≈ 19 % (0.8 dB). If the 1.03 is a deliberate GV calibration, pass classes="legacy" or ask for it to become the default.

science2.3 Velocity–diameter filter rebuilt from its rule

0.50.0
A hand-entered 32 × 32 matrix. It follows "velocity centre within ±50 % of Vt(D)" except for one isolated accepted cell (9.5 mm drops at 4.4 m/s) and two cells at 13.6 m/s. Its Vt was linearly extrapolated above 5.8 mm, reaching 10.6 m/s at 25 mm.
0.65.0
The mask is computed: keep a cell when the velocity class centre lies within (1 ± velocity_filter) × Vt(D) (default ±50 %) and the diameter class centre is below max_diameter_mm (default 10 mm, as before). Vt is Gunn & Kinzer (1949), interpolated from a bundled 0.2 mm table, with a Stokes D² tail below 0.1 mm and the 9.175 m/s plateau above 5.9 mm. classes="legacy" keeps the old matrix, minus the isolated cell. velocity_filter=None disables the filter.

science2.4 σm was a variance

0.50.0
Sigma_M = Σ(D−Dm)² D³ N / Σ D³ N, without the square root, stored in mm² as mass_spectrum_width. Values above 20 were set to 0.
0.65.0
σm = √(…) in mm, the standard deviation of the mass spectrum (Ulbrich & Atlas 1998; Williams et al. 2014), NaN when there are no drops. The 0-substitution clip is gone, as is the same clip on Dm.

science2.5 dBZ, Dm, Dmax for dry and weak minutes

0.50.0
dBZ = max(10 log₁₀ Z, 0): Z = 0 gave 0 dBZ, and every real value below 0 dBZ (drizzle) was clamped to 0. Dm, Dmax and σm were 0 with no drops.
0.65.0
dBZ is NaN for Z = 0 and negative values are kept. Shape parameters are NaN when undefined. Extensive quantities (counts, concentration, LWC, Z, R) are 0 for an observed dry minute and NaN for an unobserved one.

science2.6 Rates use the sampled time

0.50.0
One-minute sums were divided by 60 s even when the minute held only part of its telegrams, for example at the edges of an outage. A minute with 3 of its 6 telegrams reported half its rain rate and concentration.
0.65.0
Each telegram gets its own measuring interval, and sample_duration (the sum over the minute's telegrams) is the Δt in N(D) and R. A lost telegram only lengthens the spacing between neighbours, always by a whole multiple of the interval. So the interval is the largest spacing that at least 80 % of the nearby spacings are whole multiples of, and that itself occurs at least three times. The 15 spacings before, after and around each telegram are tried; the answer explaining the most spacings wins and may not exceed the telegram's own spacing. Only intervals of 10 s or more are considered, the shortest the Parsivel offers. A telegram whose own neighbourhood is inconclusive (a stamp a few seconds late, a short irregular burst) takes the interval of the nearest telegram, within 30, that has one. A day-level median, used in the first draft, doubles once half the telegrams are lost; this rule gives the right answer at 60 % random loss, follows a 60 s → 10 s change of logger interval, and stays exact at 10, 30 and 60 s intervals when stamps are up to 5 s late and 30 % of the telegrams are lost. With nothing nearby to go on, the most common interval of the surrounding telegrams is used, then the previous day's, then that of later telegrams, then 60 s with a warning. telegram_interval_s overrides the inference.

science2.7 Model soundings (Skew-T)

Where
src/gv_tools/util/skewt.py, src/gv_tools/util/upper_air.py
Grid-relative winds
RAP, HRRR and NAM store u/v relative to their Lambert-conformal grid, and 0.50.0 plotted them as if they were east/north. At Wallops the HRRR grid is turned about 14° from true north. 0.65.0 reads uvRelativeToGrid, LoV and Latin1/2 from the GRIB and rotates the winds (NCEP convention: ue = cos α u + sin α v, ve = −sin α u + cos α v, α = n(λ − LoV)).
Below-ground levels
Model isobaric levels below the ground (p ≥ psfc) are extrapolated values, yet 0.50.0 started the "surface" parcel from the highest-pressure level. The downloader now also requests PRES (surface pressure) and SPFH. Underground levels are dropped (below_ground="drop"), and the surface (2 m T/RH, 10 m wind) becomes the first level when present, so the parcel starts at the ground. The parcel label says where it starts.
Humidity
Dew point comes from specific humidity when the file has it. That avoids the ambiguity of GFS relative humidity, which is reported over ice below −20 °C.
CAPE/CIN
Unchanged on purpose. MetPy ≥ 1.6 (the package minimum) already applies the virtual-temperature correction inside cape_cin; the plot label now says so.

science2.8 Wind roses: calms

0.50.0
Speed-0 observations were binned by their reported direction, usually 0° (north), which inflated the northerly sector.
0.65.0
Speeds below calm_threshold (0.5 m/s, the usual calm limit) count toward the total but are reported as "Calm x %" rather than placed in a sector, so sectors plus calm add up to 100 %. The AIO quicklook also draws wind direction as points, which removes the false vertical strokes at every 0°/360° wrap.

science2.9 Parsivel quicklook

0.65.0
N(D) spans several decades, so it is now coloured on a log scale (log_scale=False restores linear). It is drawn on the true class edges, and minutes without telegrams are shaded grey so outages aren't read as dry weather.

science2.10 Telegrams and gauge reports go in the minute they describe

0.50.0
Telegrams were binned by their logger stamp. A Parsivel telegram holds the drops of the interval that ends at its stamp, so a 60 s telegram stamped 12:01:00 (covering 12:00–12:01) was labelled 12:01, one minute late. The first draft of 0.65.0 did the same for tipping-bucket reports.
0.65.0
A Parsivel telegram is counted in the minute holding the centre of its interval (stamp − interval/2). A gauge report covers the time since the previous report and goes in the minute whose centre it covers. Minutes are labelled by their start, so the 12:01:00 telegram now fills 12:00. For 10 s telegrams stamped at :05, :15 … nothing moves. time_binning="stamp" restores stamp binning for Parsivel.

3. The nine 0.50.0 issues from the gv_process README

fix1. Raw PIERS and APU Parsivel files could not be read on their own

Was
PIERS0042_Parsivel_20260810001005.zip raised "could not infer a date". apu04_2026081000.zip raised "contain no valid observations". A folder of them silently returned nothing.
Now
File names are no longer needed. Each source (a file or archive member) is scanned for its earliest and latest telegram stamp; this only looks at the 14 bytes before each ; and costs about 0.05 s for a day of packets. Sources are then decoded in that order. Raw PIERS and APU names, undated names and multi-day files all work, and discover_parsivel reports the dates actually present. filename_stamp() still recognises 8-, 10-, 12- and 14-digit stamps; it is used only to skip packet and daily files more than a day outside a start_date/end_date window when a directory is scanned. Files you name explicitly are never skipped by name.
Files
_parsivel.py, io/readers.py, adapters/parsivel.py

fix2. A day lost its last minutes

Was
Only files named for a date were read, and only telegrams stamped with that date were kept. The PIERS packet stamped 00:00:05 the next morning holds 23:50–23:59, so neither day kept those minutes.
Now
Every telegram goes to the UTC day (and minute) of the centre of its measuring interval. A day is produced only once no unread source can hold a telegram for it: the earliest stamp of every remaining source must lie at least 30 minutes after the end of the day. A file named for one day that also holds part of an earlier day can therefore no longer produce that earlier day a second time and replace it with a partial copy. That was a defect in the first draft, found in review. To get one complete day from read_parsivel, pass that day's folder and the next day's (its first packet holds 23:50–23:59) with start_date/end_date.

fix3. Minutes with no telegram read as dry

Was
resample("1min").sum().fillna(0) turned outages into 0 drops and 0 mm/h.
Now
Minutes with no telegram are NaN, with telegram_count = 0 and sample_duration = 0. Day products cover all 1440 minutes. read_parsivel spans the first to last minute with a telegram and fills skipped days as missing.

fix4. Telegrams counted twice or lost

Repeats across files
Telegrams are keyed by time stamp and counted once. Exact repeats show in duplicate_telegrams. Equal stamps with different spectra show in conflicting_telegrams; the first is kept, and daily files are read first.
Daily file + packets
Reading both is now safe (same result as either alone, see test).
Logged twice
The Parsivel can't send telegrams less than 10 s apart, so an equal spectrum stamped within 2 s of another is the same telegram logged twice. It is counted once and shows in duplicate_telegrams. Before, every repeat added its drops and its sampling time. A pair that fills a gap of about two intervals is kept: that is a telegram stamped late, landing just before the next one.
Clock not set
A telegram stamped before 2005 or more than 30 days ahead of the computer's clock comes from a logger whose clock wasn't set (2000-01-01 is typical). It is rejected and counted in rejected_lines, so it can't become a day of its own or stretch read_parsivel's time axis back 26 years.
Equal member names
Archives are read in memory, member by member, so equal names in two archives no longer overwrite each other.
No final newline
Each file is read separately; nothing is concatenated. Two telegrams that are already run together on one line are split at their YYYYMMDDhhmmss; stamps.
Stray "
Lines are split by hand, with no CSV quoting. A quote can only break the telegram it sits in, and only if it falls in the spectrum.
Non-UTF-8 byte
Decoded with replacement, so a bad byte invalidates at most its own telegram (rejected_lines). Rejected lines are reported for every day a file's stamps cover, even when none of its telegrams could be used.
Validation
A telegram must have a valid 14-digit stamp and exactly 9 header fields plus 1024 non-negative integer counts, with an optional trailing comma. 0.50.0 accepted any line with at least 1034 fields, so a telegram with 10 header fields was read with every count one diameter class off. 0.50.0 also turned any unparsable count into 0. Rejected layouts produce one warning listing the field counts seen. For a differently configured logger, set spectrum_offset (header fields) or strict_fields=False.

fix5. Scratch files in the working directory

Was
./tmp/<instrument>/<date> under the current directory; two processes on the same instrument and date deleted each other's files.
Now
No files are written. temp_root is accepted but ignored, with a DeprecationWarning. The deprecated helpers unzip_files_parallel/concatenate_files use a private tempfile.mkdtemp directory when no temp_root is given, prefix member names with their archive name, and add missing newlines.

fix6. source carried absolute server paths

Now
source describes the input (for example "OTT Parsivel2 raw telegrams (PIERS logger)"), and source_files lists file names only. This applies to Parsivel, R.M. Young, Met One, WS800, gauge attrs, MRR (mrr_source_files) and 2DVD (source_file). Gauge rows keep their source_path column, which you can drop.

fix7. WS800 enthalpy and density carried mixing-ratio and pressure labels

Now
Each file is checked against the thermodynamics. Mixing ratio (g/kg) and moist enthalpy, h = 1.006 T + w(2501 + 1.86 T) kJ/kg, are computed from T, RH and p, and the MixingRatio_* values are matched to the closer one. A RelPress_* median of 0.5–2 is air density; 300–1200 is pressure. Columns holding enthalpy or density become specific_enthalpy[_min|_max…] (kJ kg⁻¹) and air_density[…] (kg m⁻³). The decision is recorded in ws800_mixing_ratio_columns/ws800_rel_press_columns. When T/RH/p are missing, the PIERS logger's known layout (enthalpy, density) is assumed. The WS800 quicklook's "Mixing ratio" panel becomes "Specific enthalpy". Units are also added for wind chill, compass and other fields that were unitless before.

fix8. read_gauge stopped at decoded packets

Now
newread_tipping_bucket(source, tip_depth_mm=…) and tipping_bucket_rain(frame, tip_depth_mm=…) return, per minute and per bucket, *_tips, *_depth_mm, *_rate_mm_h and *_flag. tip_depth_mm is required, so a bucket size is never assumed.
  • A decrease can mean three things: a counter reset (the new value counts the tips since, flag 1); a dip, where the records until the counter is back at its old level are bad (a dropout to 0, a stale value); or a spike, where the record before was bad. Each reading implies a rain rate, and a reading that needs more than max_rate_mm_h (with a Poisson allowance for bursts) is impossible. A small drop, to more than half the old level, takes the calmest reading. A drop to less than half the old level looks like a cleared counter, so it stays a reset unless the reset would need a rate more than three times the dip's plus two tips per report. Removed records get flag 16.
  • Examples. Plain differencing turned 100, 101, 102, 3, 103 into 106 tips and a single 9999 into 2 541 mm; now the first gives 3 tips and the 9999 is removed. Light rain across a reset (1, 2, 0, 1, 2, 3) and a reboot in heavy rain (500, 515, 0, 15, 30) count every tip. A dry evening with a two-record dropout (15, 15, 0, 0, 15) gives 0 tips, and a stale value after a reset (0, 3, 5, 4, 7, 8) gives 8.
  • A dropout longer than three records is recognised when the counter jumps back to its old level. The records since the drop are removed and the tips count from the old level (1013, 1013, 0, 0, 0, 0, 1016, 1018 gives 5 tips).
  • A drop on the last record can't be confirmed by a later one, and a jump no rain can make is a re-based counter. Their tips are NaN (flag 1) rather than the raw counter value. With counter_max, a decrease counts as a wrap-around only if the implied tips are plausible at max_rate_mm_h, so a logger restart from 40 002 to 0 is not 25 534 tips.
  • A logger that clears its counter on a schedule should use glitch_window=0, which reads every drop as a reset (see §10).
  • A report covers the time since the previous one and goes in the minute whose centre it covers (§2.10). Minutes no report covers are NaN: before the first report (or before a start filter), inside gaps longer than 2.5× the usual spacing (flag 2: tips to the gap's last minute, rate NaN), and inside a report spanning several minutes in which tips fell (flag 8). Tips already counted in a minute that such a report makes unknown move with its tips, so none are lost. A multi-minute report with no tips makes all its minutes 0, because nothing fell.
  • Rates above max_rate_mm_h (300) are flagged (4) and their depth and rate set to NaN, so sums can't include them by accident. The tips are kept.
counter="incremental" handles loggers that report tips per record. read_gauge also reads daily files and packets together now, dropping exact repeats (duplicate_records).
Note
gvp_tipping_bucket.py was not in the zip, so this is a new implementation rather than a move. It treats TB_*_tips as a cumulative counter, which matches the README's mention of counter resets.

fix9. h5netcdf range too old for xarray 2026.9

Now
The netcdf and all extras require h5netcdf>=1.8, and gv-tools check reports 1.8 as the minimum. If an older h5netcdf is installed anyway, read_mrr raises an ImportError that names the fix instead of "'Variable' object has no attribute 'filters'".

6. Performance and memory

Measured for the reviewed 0.60.0 candidate whose implementation is released here as 0.65.0, using the same synthetic PIERS day: 145 ten-minute zip packets, 10 s telegrams, 8 640 telegrams. 0.50.0 could only read it after the files were renamed to 8-digit dates. Best of 3 runs, repeated on different runs of a shared machine (hence the ranges); peak from tracemalloc. These supplied benchmarks were reviewed but not rerun during the 0.65.0 integration.

9. Compatibility and API changes

  • Kept signatures: _parsivel.run, process_input, load_tables, get_integral_parameters, get_dataset_from_parsivel and discover_input_dates still accept their 0.50.0 arguments. run now also accepts a list of files. load_tables() returns the 0.50.0 layout (32 × 33 mask) built from the OTT classes; pass classes="legacy" or table_dir to get the old tables. If gv_process calls load_tables() + get_integral_parameters(), it picks up the corrections automatically.
  • Changed meaning: mass_spectrum_width is now in mm (σm), not mm². radar_reflectivity can be NaN or negative.
  • Changed shape: Parsivel day products cover 1440 minutes; read_parsivel covers the observed span, with missing minutes inside it.
  • New columns in the Parsivel parameter frame: Nw, V, Raw Drops, Telegrams, Sample_Seconds.
  • Changed minute labels: with time_binning="centre" (default), telegrams whose stamps fall on or just after the minute (for example 60 s telegrams at :00 or :13) are labelled one minute earlier than in 0.50.0. Gauge reports are likewise labelled by the minute they cover.
  • Deprecated: temp_root (ignored), unzip_files_parallel, concatenate_files.
  • Removed: the "order files from earliest to latest" error and the symlink staging directory in read_parsivel.
  • Dependencies: h5netcdf>=1.8 in the netcdf/all extras. No new required packages.

10. Assumptions and limits to know about

  • Telegram layout — please check one real PIERS file and one real APU file. 9 header fields precede the 1024 spectrum values, as in 0.50.0, and only a trailing comma may follow the spectrum. If the loggers write a field after the spectrum, every telegram is rejected and the warning names the field count it saw. Then pass strict_fields=False, which ignores fields after the spectrum, or spectrum_offset if the header has a different number of fields.
  • Day edges. A day file's first telegram can belong to the previous day: a 60 s telegram stamped 00:00:13 covers 23:59:13–00:00:13 and is counted at 23:59. The day's own last minutes are in the next day's first file. Pass the next day's folder as well to get a complete day.
  • Time convention. 0.65.0 assumes the logger stamps a telegram (or gauge report) at the end of the interval it describes, which is how Parsivel telegrams and PIERS packets work. If a logger stamps the start instead, use time_binning="stamp" for Parsivel.
  • Interval inference. Right after a change of logger interval, one telegram can get the old interval, because measuring windows that would overlap are ambiguous. A run of fewer than about 20 telegrams at a longer interval inside a stream at a shorter one (ten 60 s telegrams in a 10 s day, say) looks exactly like lost telegrams and gets the shorter interval. A day with only two or three widely spaced telegrams can't show its interval, so the neighbouring days' interval is used, then 60 s with a warning. Pass telegram_interval_s when you know the interval.
  • Date windows and file names. With start_date/end_date, packet files and *_daily files named more than a day outside the window are skipped without being opened. Files named with a plain 8-digit date are always scanned, because such a file can hold several days; scanning costs about 0.1 s per 100 MB. A logger whose clock jumps to a wrong but possible date (a single telegram stamped on the wrong day, say) still produces that day.
  • Terminal speed. Gunn & Kinzer values are for still air near sea level. They are right for Wallops; a high-altitude site would need an air-density correction.
  • Tipping buckets — please check how the PIERS logger handles TB_*_tips. The processor assumes a cumulative counter. Confirm this against gvp_tipping_bucket.py, or send it and it can replace this implementation.
    • Counter never cleared, or cleared daily or on a reboot. The defaults suit this case. In tests with 1 % stale values, 1 % spikes and 0.5 % dropouts, totals were within 0.4 %.
    • Counter cleared on a schedule (every packet, every hour). Pass glitch_window=0. With small counts a reset and a dropout look alike. With the counter cleared every 10 minutes in showery rain, the default rules counted 6 % fewer tips and glitch_window=0 2 % fewer. That 2 % is the tips hidden by resets that show no drop (the first count after the reset at or above the old level), which no reading of the counter can recover.
    A wrong but plausible first value can't be told from rain.
  • Size classes. The default (OTT) gives about 9 % less rain than 0.50.0. Choose classes="legacy" if the 1.03 factor is an intended calibration.
  • HRRR downloads. The NOMADS HRRR filter endpoint (filter_hrrr_2d.pl with wrfprs files) is unchanged and couldn't be tested from here.
  • User code. Your parsivel_fx.py uses the same tables and may contain the same velocity-axis pattern. Check it before comparing its output with gv_tools.
