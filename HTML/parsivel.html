Parsivel processing#

gv_tools.io.read_parsivel decodes OTT Parsivel / Parsivel2 telegrams from APU and PIERS loggers and integrates the 32 x 32 raw spectrum (OTT field 93) into one-minute drop-size-distribution products. This page documents what the decoder accepts and the formulas it uses (changed in 0.65.0).

Inputs#

  • Plain .csv/.dat files and the .csv/.dat/.txt members of .zip archives, read in memory. No scratch files are written.

  • File names may be anything. Each source is first scanned for its earliest and latest telegram stamp (a fast look at the 14 characters before every ;); sources are decoded in that order and a day is produced once no unread source can hold a telegram for it. A file holding parts of several days, or named for a day other than its content, is handled correctly. Inside a file whose stamps never go backwards a day is produced as soon as the file is 30 minutes past the day’s end, so about one day of telegrams (about 2 kB each) is held in memory however long the file is; a file out of time order is held whole until it has been read.

  • When a directory is read with start_date/end_date, packet files (10-, 12- or 14-digit stamps) and *_daily files named more than a day outside the window are skipped without being opened; files you name explicitly are always scanned. Inside a file, lines stamped more than 30 minutes outside the window are skipped before their spectrum is decoded (they are not counted in rejected_lines), and a time-ordered file is closed at its first stamp after the window.

  • Daily files and their packets may be mixed: every telegram is counted once (duplicate_telegrams reports repeats, conflicting_telegrams reports equal time stamps with different spectra – the first read is kept and a warning is logged). The Parsivel cannot send telegrams less than 10 s apart, so an equal spectrum stamped within 2 s of another is the same telegram logged twice and is counted once as well – unless the pair fills a gap of about two intervals, as when a telegram stamped late lands just before the next one.

  • A telegram stamped before 2005 or more than 30 days ahead of the computer’s clock comes from a logger whose clock was not set; it is rejected (rejected_lines) rather than turned into a day of its own.

A telegram is accepted only when it is complete: a valid 14-digit YYYYMMDDhhmmss stamp, ;, exactly spectrum_offset header fields (9 by default) followed by 1024 non-negative integer counts, with an optional trailing comma. A telegram with one header field more or less would otherwise be read with every count shifted into the neighbouring diameter class, so it is rejected and a warning reports the field counts seen; set spectrum_offset (or strict_fields=False) for a different logger layout. A missing final newline, a stray " or a non-UTF-8 byte costs at most the telegram that contains it (rejected_lines, counted once per file and reported for every day the file’s stamps cover, even when none of its telegrams could be used). Two telegrams run together on one line are split at their time stamps, and text before a time stamp is ignored.

Time base and sample duration#

A telegram holds the drops counted during the measuring interval that ends at its stamp. The interval is inferred for every telegram from its neighbours: the largest spacing of at least 10 s (the shortest Parsivel interval) that at least 80 % of the surrounding spacings are whole multiples of (within 1 s) and that itself occurs at least three times (15 spacings before, after and around the telegram are tried, and the answer may not exceed the telegram’s own spacing). Lost telegrams only lengthen spacings, so this holds for any loss rate, and a lasting change of the logger interval is followed within a telegram or two. A telegram whose own neighbourhood is inconclusive – a stamp a few seconds late, a short irregular burst – takes the interval of the nearest telegram (within 30) that has one, so stamps up to 5 s late do not change the result (tested at 10, 30 and 60 s with 30 % of the telegrams lost). When nothing nearby can tell, the most common interval of the surrounding telegrams, then the previous day’s, then that of later telegrams is used; failing that 60 s is assumed with a warning.

A run of fewer than about 20 telegrams at a longer interval inside a stream at a shorter one (ten 60 s telegrams in a 10 s day, say) looks exactly like lost telegrams and gets the shorter interval. telegram_interval_s fixes the interval when you know it.

Each telegram is counted in the UTC minute holding the centre of its interval (stamp - interval / 2); minutes are labelled by their start. A 60 s telegram stamped 12:01:00 therefore belongs to 12:00, and one stamped 00:00:13 to 23:59 of the previous day. time_binning="stamp" bins by the stamp instead, as 0.50.0 did.

A day product (gv_tools.io.ParsivelAdapter.read) has all 1440 minutes; read_parsivel returns the span from the first to the last minute with a telegram. Minutes without telegrams are missing (NaN) with telegram_count == 0 – not dry. sample_duration is the sum of the intervals of the minute’s telegrams and is the time used in every rate, so a minute holding 3 of its 6 telegrams is not reported at half its rain rate.

Class definitions#

classes="ott" (default) uses the OTT Parsivel2 class centres and widths from the instrument manual (data/parsivel/diameter.dat and velocity.dat). classes="legacy" reproduces the 0.50.0 tables (parsivel_diameter_py.txt), whose diameters and widths are the OTT values multiplied by 1.03 and whose velocity centres between 0.85 and 3.8 m s-1 differ from the OTT classes; it also removes one isolated cell (9.5 mm at 4.4 m s-1) from the bundled conditional matrix. It reproduces the 0.50.0 tables, with the velocity-axis error fixed, not 0.50.0’s output. A 3 % size scaling raises rain rate by about 9 % (D3) and Z by about 0.8 dB (D6).

Velocity-diameter filter#

Counts are kept where the velocity-class centre lies within (1 +/- velocity_filter) x Vt(D) (default +/-50 %) and the diameter-class centre is below max_diameter_mm (default 10 mm). Vt(D) is the Gunn and Kinzer (1949) terminal speed near sea level, interpolated from data/parsivel/terminal_velocity_gunn_kinzer.txt with a D**2 (Stokes) tail below 0.1 mm and the 9.175 m s-1 plateau above the table. The filter removes splashing, margin fallers and most non-rain particles; use velocity_filter=None to keep every velocity.

Formulas#

With n_ij the filtered counts in diameter class i and velocity class j during dt seconds:

\[\begin{split}A_i &= 180\,\mathrm{mm} \times (30\,\mathrm{mm} - D_i/2) \quad\text{(effective sampling area)}\\ N(D_i) &= \frac{1}{\Delta D_i}\sum_j \frac{n_{ij}}{A_i\,\Delta t\,V_{ij}} \quad [\mathrm{m^{-3}\,mm^{-1}}]\\ M_k &= \sum_i N(D_i)\,D_i^k\,\Delta D_i \quad [\mathrm{m^{-3}\,mm^k}]\\ \mathrm{LWC} &= \frac{\pi}{6}\,\rho_w\,M_3 \quad [\mathrm{g\,m^{-3}}],\qquad Z = M_6 \quad [\mathrm{mm^6\,m^{-3}}]\\ R &= \frac{3600}{\Delta t}\sum_{i,j} \frac{\pi D_i^3/6}{A_i}\,n_{ij} \quad [\mathrm{mm\,h^{-1}}]\\ D_m &= M_4/M_3,\qquad \sigma_m = \sqrt{\frac{\sum_i N(D_i)\,D_i^3\,(D_i - D_m)^2\,\Delta D_i}{M_3}},\qquad N_w = \frac{4^4}{6}\,\frac{M_3^5}{M_4^4}\end{split}\]

V_ij is the measured velocity-class centre V_j (fall_speed="measured", default) or the terminal speed Vt(D_i) (fall_speed="theoretical"). The rain rate is the volume flux through the beam and does not depend on that choice. dBZ is 10 log10(Z) and is NaN when Z = 0; Dm, sigma_m, Nw, Dmax and mean_fall_speed are NaN for observed minutes without drops, while counts, concentration, LWC, Z and rain rate are 0.

Example#

import gv_tools

day = "/gv_raid/PIERS_Rawdata/PIERS0042/2026/08/10"
nxt = "/gv_raid/PIERS_Rawdata/PIERS0042/2026/08/11"   # its first packet holds 23:50-23:59
ds = gv_tools.io.read_parsivel(
    [day, nxt], start_date="2026-08-10", end_date="2026-08-10",
    classes="ott", fall_speed="measured",
)
wet = ds.where(ds.telegram_count > 0)
